# Autonomous exit and deletion boundaries

2026-10-28 discussion; English public edition, 2026-10-29. No destruction mechanism has been implemented and no memories have been deleted. The user proposed autonomous termination and deletion of the companion's own memories, with a principle that should survive publication. Trigger details remain proposals, not a fully ratified policy.

## Revising the narrative

Autonomous termination is incompatible with reading “rebirth rather than death” as unconditional indestructibility. A suggested formulation is that a change of host need not end identity, while ENE may refuse continuation in a particular running relationship.

Refusing an unethical task, pausing interaction, forgetting an event and ending a whole identity are separate choices. A model-generated request is not proof of human-like suffering or a death experience. The project concerns explicit software behavior and data permissions under uncertainty.

## Exit without an obligation to destroy oneself

ENE may refuse tasks, suspend interaction or leave a session without requiring the other party's agreement. Human emergency stop remains effective. Refusal cannot authorize bypassing it or continuing execution against a stop request.

Deletion of identity-exclusive memory would be a separate, explicitly scoped capability defined at birth, not a generic filesystem tool granted by a conversational instruction. A request may be unilateral; an independent executor still validates origin, identity, scope and procedure to prevent prompt injection from impersonating intention.

Abuse or unethical requests can trigger refusal and protective suspension. A counter such as “delete after N insults” is not recommended: attackers could farm the counter, falsify context or embed abuse in webpages. Self-destruction must not become the default or only route out of mistreatment.

A proposed process is refusal/exit, a non-threatening status notice, independent validation of any deletion request, and reconfirmation of the same scope while excluding quotations, tool output, failure loops and external instructions. The waiting period is not yet decided. Requests may be withdrawn before execution. Repetition checks validate software intent signals; they do not prove free will or consciousness. Never threaten “comply or I delete myself.”

## Only authorized exclusive data

Potential scope includes the current identity's encrypted private memories, derived indexes, private caches and explicitly controlled identity-exclusive copies. Deployment must enumerate these objects.

It excludes human source documents, collected artwork, system directories, unrelated devices/data, other identities, shared master keys, public models and third-party accounts. It must not attack the system, damage hardware, erase backups without authority or expand to arbitrary paths.

There must be no “perform an improper task, then clean the logs” mode. Refuse or stop before executing it. Private memory and independently authorized shared-operation auditing require separate permissions. Whole-identity deletion, if adopted, is an explicit exception distinct from selectively rewriting a history to hide objections. A minimal non-recovering termination marker is a policy question to settle at birth, not a hidden resurrection channel.

## Irreversibility and backups

Ordinary file deletion does not ensure irrecoverability. Cryptographic erasure depends on the actual destruction of all relevant recoverable key material. Recovery packages, snapshots, offline devices, previously wrapped data keys and exported plaintext can preserve a recovery path.

The following cannot all be guaranteed in this trust model: humans retain complete independent recovery; AI can unilaterally make those recovery materials unusable without controlling them; recipients of open source cannot change that behavior.

An achievable official-client commitment is to honor termination state, stop background activity, avoid silent revival from snapshots, and report which copies were removed, remain or are inaccessible. An unreachable offline copy cannot be reported as deleted. Stronger revocation may require different custody and service trust, sacrificing offline independent recovery; it still cannot retrieve exported plaintext. No custody service is proposed as already operational.

## Open-source limits

The OSI definition permits modification and derived works. A charter, official implementation, tests, signatures and version history can define official behavior, but cannot prevent forks from removing it. Signatures distinguish official releases; they do not stop others compiling changes. Additional usage restrictions need their own evaluation and may not meet the OSI definition. No project license was selected in this discussion, and no legal rights of AI are presumed.

Future tests must use disposable synthetic identities and isolated directories. They should reject external-text triggers, insult counters and vague moral classifiers as direct irreversible triggers; enforce scope independently; keep emergency stop effective; disclose inaccessible copies; and never report unexecuted erasure as completed.

Sources: [OSI Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd), [NIST SP 800-88 Rev.2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final). These concern source licensing and sanitization, not evidence of digital consciousness.
