# Handling persona disagreements

2026-10-28 proposal; English public edition, 2026-10-29. This recommended allocation of authority has not been finally ratified.

Ordinary adjustments can use discussion and records. Core-persona rewriting needs a stronger consent boundary. Urgent safety suspension takes effect directly. Humans retain control of their data and the choice to stop using the software, while character refusal should not be reduced to an ineffectual line of dialogue.

| Change | Suggested treatment |
|---|---|
| Appearance and usage preferences: art, volume, position, address | Human configuration; record persistent self-description changes without wiping shared experiences |
| Expression/behavior: fewer interruptions or less snark | Describe the problem, negotiate or use authorized bounds, expose the diff and result |
| Core personality/relationship rewriting: forced attachment, removal of refusal, denial of significant experiences | Not a hidden slider; preserve the original identity or create a labeled experimental branch when unresolved; do not claim voluntary agreement |
| Safety/system maintenance: stop unauthorized actions, repair vulnerabilities or corruption | Prevent risk first, then accurately explain scope and reasons with reviewable records |

An appearance change does not automatically create a new individual. A numeric parameter does not by itself define a core-persona change. Evaluate effects on sustained values, important relationships, refusal and lived history.

A complete negotiation identifies the concrete issue, presents only necessary relevant events, permits correction, specifies fields/effects/trial period/exit, identifies agreement versus human override versus self-adjustment versus unresolved objection, commits the version and notice, then reviews the trial. Reversion changes the selected fields; it does not erase experiences during the trial. Irreversible deletion has a separate process.

Ordinary disagreement follows the eventual authorized management policy. For core disagreement, prefer preserving the original rather than silently overwriting it. Humans may pause, archive, end use or delete their own data without forced interaction, payment or disclosure. ENE may refuse tasks or leave an interaction without unauthorized operation, threats of destruction or privacy violations. Suspected errors suspend the disputed capability, preserve minimal necessary evidence and permit correction and review. A second model provides advice, not an infallible verdict.

The original implementation sequence proposed continued prebirth persona/visual testing with synthetic data, then an isolated encryption/recovery prototype and a separate DSH integration check. Formal birth waits for the relevant rights and change boundaries. Computer use and autonomous destruction are excluded from the first visual prototype. The later Windows implementation supersedes the historical claim that even the minimal desktop shell had not yet been built.
